Exercise 4 - Parallel Nested Loops and collapse(2)

Part 1 - Without collapse(2)

The #pragma omp parallel for directive is applied only to the outer
loop. Therefore, the five iterations of the r loop are distributed
among the available threads.

For each assigned value of r, the same thread executes all ten
iterations of the inner k loop. Consequently, all values from k = 1
to k = 10 for a particular value of r are normally processed by the
same thread.

Since five outer-loop iterations are distributed among four threads,
one thread may receive two values of r while the other threads receive
one value each. The exact mapping can depend on the OpenMP runtime and
its scheduling policy.

Part 2 - With collapse(2)

The collapse(2) clause combines the two nested loops into a single
iteration space. The five r iterations and ten k iterations produce
a total of 5 x 10 = 50 combined iterations.

These 50 combined iterations are distributed across the four threads.
This provides a finer-grained and more balanced distribution of work
than parallelizing only the five iterations of the outer loop.

With the default static scheduling, the threads will normally receive
approximately equal amounts of work. For 50 iterations and four
threads, two threads normally receive 13 iterations each and the
remaining two threads receive 12 iterations each.

The output order is not sequential because the threads execute their
assigned iterations concurrently.
