Exercise 8 - Molecular Dynamics Using OpenMP

The molecular dynamics force calculation evaluates the Lennard-Jones
interaction between pairs of particles in a box with periodic boundary
conditions.

Parallelization

The outer particle loop is parallelized using:

#pragma omp parallel for schedule(runtime)
    reduction(+:potential, virial)

The potential energy and virial are the two reduction variables.
OpenMP gives each thread private partial values and combines them
safely at the end of the parallel loop. This prevents data races on
the accumulated energy values.

Critical Directive

A pair interaction updates the forces of both particle i and particle
j. Different outer-loop iterations may update the same particle at
the same time. Therefore, the shared force array is protected using:

#pragma omp critical(force_update)

This ensures that only one thread updates the shared force array at a
time and prevents lost or corrupted force values.

Correctness

The serial reference produced:

Potential energy = -3200.5886426909
Virial           = -16936.4179779411

The parallel implementation was tested with 1, 2, 3, and 4 threads.
Every execution passed verification. The maximum force differences
were approximately 10^-15, while the small differences in potential
and virial were caused by the different floating-point addition order
used during parallel reduction.

Thread-count Performance

1 thread:
Time    = 0.020956 seconds
Speedup = 0.96x

2 threads:
Time    = 0.007380 seconds
Speedup = 2.73x

3 threads:
Time    = 0.014294 seconds
Speedup = 1.41x

4 threads:
Time    = 0.019308 seconds
Speedup = 1.04x

The two-thread execution gave the best result in this test. Increasing
the number of threads did not always improve performance because the
critical region serializes shared force updates. Thread-management
overhead and the short execution time also affect the measurements.

For this execution, static,16 produced the best measured time. The
performance difference is caused by the balance between scheduling
overhead, workload distribution, and critical-section contention.
Timing results may vary between executions and systems.
